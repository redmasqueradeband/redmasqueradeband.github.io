I've been doing board-level repair for about twenty years, mostly industrial control gear, some consumer stuff on the side. I don't post here often but I don't know where else to put this.

Pulled a board out of a job lot I bought sight-unseen from a liquidation auction. No label, no case, nothing plugged in. It's been sitting on my bench for three days and it is glowing. Not reflecting light — glowing, on its own, a dim violet color, worse in the dark. I ran continuity on every trace I can access, blind, no assumptions going in. Nothing reads as a closed circuit. There is no battery on this board. I have never seen anything like this in two decades of doing this.

glow_board_kilovolt1.jpg

The glow isn't constant either. It breathes. Gets slightly brighter, dims, repeats, on a cycle of maybe 4-5 seconds. I timed it with a stopwatch because I didn't believe myself the first time. If I had to describe the tone that comes with it — and I know how this sounds — it's closer to a drone note than a hum. Sustained, not electrical.

Electroluminescent coating is my first guess, some prototype boards used it for status indication before status LEDs were standardized/cheap. Would explain the glow without a closed circuit if it's capacitive rather than resistive. Wouldn't explain the "breathing" though, that's usually driven logic, not a passive coating.
